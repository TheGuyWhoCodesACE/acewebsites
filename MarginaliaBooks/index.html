Staff picks

What we're handing people this week

Four of us read for the front table. If a book sits here, one of us finished it and will defend it near the register.

Dana · fiction

The Dispossessed — Ursula K. Le Guin

Fifty years old and still the sharpest argument in novel form about what we owe each other. I reread it every election year.

Paperback $18 · On the front table

Omar · history

The Warmth of Other Suns — Isabel Wilkerson

Three people, one migration, no footnotes fighting for attention. The rare history book my non-reading brother finished in a week.

Paperback $20 · Last three on the shelf

June · kids & YA

The First Rule of Punk — Celia C. Pérez

Twelve, angry, funny, and makes a case for making things badly on purpose. Hand it to any kid who says they're bored.

Hardcover $17 · Story hour shelf

Theo · everything else

How to Do Nothing — Jenny Odell

Not a productivity book, whatever the airport thinks. About attention as something worth defending. I sell two a week and have never once been wrong about the buyer.

Paperback $16 · By the register

The shop

Open since 1994, arguing since 1995

Marginalia started in half a laundromat with four shelves and a cat named Colophon. We're on Bell Street now — two rooms, a back table that hosts clubs and zine night, and a poetry section that keeps growing whether we plan it or not. The cat is retired; the arguing continues.

  • Independent since day one, no chain ownership
  • Every staff pick is a book we finished ourselves
  • Local authors get a shelf by the door, free
  • Used trade-ins welcome during open hours
